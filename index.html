2025 – presentPedagogy

Fostering Curiosity

The previous IB evaluation asked SVP to deepen inquiry and conceptual understanding, and teacher feedback pointed at student agency and self-regulation. I built a school-wide programme on ACER's research on curiosity, in three strands: fostering curiosity, focusing it, and resolving knowledge gaps. From the start it was designed to be refined as it ran, and teachers and students both report on it every term.

The termly cycle

  1. 1TeachTeachers choose strategies from the shared bank and log them in the strategy tracker.
  2. 2GaugeStudents rate their curiosity on the Curiosity Thermometer in lessons and complete a Curiosity Transcript at the end of each unit.
  3. 3ReflectStudents write termly curiosity reflections with their homeroom advisor. Teachers reflect on which strategies worked and why.
  4. 4ReviewSubject coordinators review the reflections and tracking data with their departments.
  5. 5AdjustDepartments change strategies, observation focus or support for the next term.

Step 5 feeds the next term's step 1. Year-end teacher reflections set the following year's focus.

How it changed

  • During the first year: reflections in Terms 3 and 4 showed curiosity was harder to sustain in demanding subjects. Departments added warm-up strategies, re-taught the Thermometer language and focused lesson observations on sustaining curiosity.
  • At year end: teachers' end-of-year reflections set the focus areas and strategies for 2026–27.
  • In the second year: tracking widened from individual strategies to students' general curiosity across subjects, and the programme became the school's Programme Development Plan.
2021 – presentProfessional learning

A professional learning system

Before 2021, professional development was a series of separate events. I replaced it with a system where the school's priority, each department's goals and each teacher's goals line up.

  • Introduced an annual pedagogical focus area for the whole secondary school, with department goals and teachers' personal and professional goals aligned to it.
  • Established Professional Learning Communities, with a "critical friend" model for peer reflection and shared practice.
  • Developed a 360-degree professional evaluation, introduced in 2026, so teachers receive feedback from colleagues, leaders and students rather than from one observer.
  • Introduced a teaching observation tool tied to the focus areas, so observation feedback reinforces what the school is working on that year.
2023 – presentCurriculum

Exit competencies and a connected curriculum

Students were arriving in the DP with conceptual and skill gaps that teachers spent early units repairing. I led the work to plan progression backwards from what each stage needs, and to put the IB core inside subjects rather than beside them.

What I did

  • Led the development of Grade 7–10 exit competencies, DP subject-specific exit competencies and school-wide macro skills, so each grade knows what the next one expects.
  • Introduced annual horizontal and vertical curriculum maps covering content, assessment, ATL skills and TOK links, reviewed each year with a formal reflection cycle.
  • Mapped TOK concepts and CAS opportunities across every DP subject, with scaffolded knowledge questions and Support Centre sessions linking CAS, EE and TOK.

Result

Grade 10 and 11 examinations and a DP orientation week now prepare students for the transition. DP subject choice widened with ESS, Design Technology, History, Psychology and Film through Pamoja. CAS moved to student-led service: the 2026 project in Dusun Temon, Gunungkidul, has 62 students restoring village infrastructure.

Grades 7 – 12ATL skills

Academic and Welfare Days, and the inquiry pathway

ATL skills were listed in unit plans but rarely taught explicitly. I introduced Academic and Welfare Days, dedicated days for teaching thinking, research, self-management and resilience directly, and built a pathway that hands students more ownership each year.

Passion Project, Grades 7–8→Service as Action, Grade 9→Personal Project, Grade 10→CAS, EE and TOK, Grades 11–12

The Passion Project starts it: Grade 7 and 8 students set their own goals and pursue questions of their own, many linked to the UN Sustainable Development Goals and partner NGOs, with teachers acting as facilitators. By the Personal Project and the Extended Essay, independent research is familiar rather than new.

2025 – presentStudent support

Multi-Tiered System of Supports

I introduced MTSS in 2025 to give every concern about a student a defined route, from classroom adjustment through to specialist intervention, and to bring the school's separate support services into one system.

  • Brought Pastoral Heads, homeroom advisors, an Inclusion Support Teacher and a four-part counselling team (educational psychology, guidance, wellbeing, careers) into one tiered structure.
  • Added targeted English and Mathematics support, DP Clinics, WIAT assessment and IEP-linked access arrangements.
  • Set up welfare monitoring and year-to-year student handovers, and learning-habits reporting across Grades 7–11 that flags students on eight learning dispositions.

Result

Students who need support are now identified earlier, and Tier 2 interventions are more effective. More inclusion students are now taking and completing the full IB Diploma.

OngoingAI and assessment

AI in teaching, learning and assessment

I translated the IB's AI principles and academic integrity guidance into practice at SVP: a traffic-light system for AI use on each task, compulsory AI declarations for Internal Assessments and core components, and subject-specific guidance for Language A, Language B, Mathematics AA and TOK. My doctoral research uses this as its case: how a leader enables teachers to make sense of a conceptual AI policy within their own subjects.

Research and qualifications

Research

My doctoral research at Edgewood University is about enabling leadership: how a school leader creates adaptive spaces in which teachers make sense of change together, rather than receiving it as instruction. It draws on Uhl-Bien, Marion and McKelvey's (2007) complexity leadership theory, in which enabling leadership connects a school's administrative structures with the adaptive work of its teachers, together with research on distributed leadership and communities of practice.

The case is artificial intelligence. The IB's AI policy is conceptual, and what it means for assessment in Mathematics is not what it means in Language A. The study asks how a leader enables teachers to work out that gap within their own subjects. The same thinking runs through my practice at SVP, where PLCs, critical friends and termly reflections are spaces of this kind.
